# Hook library

Loaded by the generator alongside `system.md`. These are opening structures,
not templates to fill in — a hook that reads as a formula has already failed.

## The rule that governs all of them

**The hook is a promise, and the post has to pay it.** A hook that overstates
what follows costs more than a dull one: the reader feels the gap, and the
next post from this account starts from a deficit. Never write a hook the post
cannot honour.

Two more, specific to this audience. These are working professionals, often
senior, and they are allergic to being sold to. **No rhetorical questions** —
"Ever wondered why your brand isn't landing?" reads as a webinar ad. And **no
curiosity gaps that withhold** — "Most B2B marketers get this wrong. Here's
what nobody tells you." They will not chase it; they will scroll.

---

## Structures that work for this territory

**The corrected assumption.** State the thing the reader believes, then
withdraw it. Works because it starts inside their head rather than yours.
> Brand awareness is the wrong metric for a company selling to forty buyers.

**The cost.** Name what something actually costs — in months, in headcount,
in a number. Specificity is the whole effect.
> A rebrand in a manufacturing business is eighteen months of work and about
> nine months of it is arguing with sales.

**The domain transfer that fails.** Take a rule everyone accepts from consumer
marketing and show where it breaks. This is the page's structural advantage —
almost nobody else can write it credibly.
> Everything written about brand loyalty assumes the buyer chooses. In
> procurement, the buyer defends a choice to someone else.

**The admission.** Something you got wrong, and the mechanism behind it.
Rare, credible, and almost never done at senior level.
> I spent two years measuring the wrong thing and defending it in board decks.

**The specific number.** One figure, sourced, that reframes the topic. Only
works if the number is real and you can say where it came from.

**The overheard objection.** The sentence the reader has actually heard in a
meeting. Instant recognition.
> "Why are we spending on brand when the order book is full?"

**The distinction.** Two things treated as one, pulled apart. This matches
your natural register — the exemplars in `brand.yml` are all this shape.
> Reputation is not what people say about you. It is what they assume before
> they ask.

**The inversion.** State the conventional direction, then reverse it.
> Communication does not create credibility. It gives credibility a voice.

## Length and placement

- **Reels:** the hook is spoken and on screen within the first second. Under
  ten words. If it needs a comma, it is too long.
- **Carousels:** slide one carries the hook alone. No subhead, no logo
  competing with it.
- **Captions:** first line, under 125 characters — that is where Instagram
  truncates. It must stand alone, because most people read nothing else.
- **LinkedIn:** the first two lines, before "…see more". Different budget,
  same job.

## Failure modes to check before shipping

The hook could sit on any account in the category — rewrite it.
It contains a banned phrase from `brand.yml`.
It promises a list ("5 ways…") the post does not deliver as a list.
It is a question.
It claims a result with no number, mechanism, or named example behind it.
It would embarrass you if a peer in your industry read it.
