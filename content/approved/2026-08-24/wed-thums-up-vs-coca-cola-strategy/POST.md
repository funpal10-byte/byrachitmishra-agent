## Caption — copy from here

```
This brand case study India shows why Coca-Cola failed to replace Thums Up in 1993 despite owning distribution.

When Coke acquired Parle's drink portfolio for $60 million, the strategy was to phase out Thums Up and shift volume to flagship Coca-Cola. Coke cut Thums Up marketing spend to zero and pulled trade incentives from distributors.

Instead of moving to Coke, Indian consumers bought Pepsi. Thums Up was formulated with higher carbonation and distinct spice top-notes (cinnamon, nutmeg, clove) tailored for heavy Indian meals. Milder Coca-Cola tasted flat by comparison.

Coke lost nearly 15 percent cola market share in two years before reversing the decision. They had to restart Thums Up bottling lines, restore trade margins, and fund its advertising again. Thums Up eventually became Coke's first billion-dollar brand originating from India.

Distribution scale forces trial, but sensory positioning creates retention.
```
