## Caption — copy from here

```
The fundamental difference between branding and marketing comes down to gross margin, not creative output.

Marketing buys immediate demand through ad spend, channel distribution, and short-term promotions. It answers a tactical question: how do we get someone to buy today?

Branding determines what you refuse to compromise on so you do not have to discount tomorrow. It is an operational discipline, not a aesthetic one.

When Indian motorcycle manufacturers in the 2000s fought over fuel economy metrics, Royal Enfield refused to optimize for mileage. They accepted lower fuel efficiency and heavier frames to preserve a distinct riding experience. That compromise protected their pricing power while competitors entered a zero-margin feature war.

Marketing buys volume. Branding protects pricing power.

If a branding decision does not cost you short-term sales or limit your initial TAM, it is just marketing with better typography.

Send this to a brand manager who is currently spending their budget on performance ads instead of positioning.
```
