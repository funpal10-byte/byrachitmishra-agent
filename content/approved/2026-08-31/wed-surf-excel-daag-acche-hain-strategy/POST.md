## Caption — copy from here

```
If you want to understand why this ad worked so well, look at how Surf Excel reframed dirt.

In 2005, the Indian detergent category was stuck in a brutal feature war. Ariel, Tide, and Nirma were all claiming superior whiteness and faster stain removal. When every competitor claims the exact same functional outcome, price becomes your only differentiator.

Surf Excel broke out by addressing the emotional tension behind the product. For parents, mud on a uniform meant extra labor and implied poor supervision. Lowe Lintas inverted that framing entirely with 'Daag Acche Hain' (Dirt is Good).

If a child got dirty while standing up for a sibling or helping a teacher, the stain became proof of good upbringing. Detergent was no longer just cleaning powder; it was the tool that removed the penalty of doing the right thing.

Great positioning does not invent new product features. It changes what the customer feels when the product is used.
```
