## Caption — copy from here

```
Here is why this ad worked when 5 Star was losing impulse market share to Snickers in India.

When a global competitor enters with a clear functional claim like hunger satisfaction, local incumbents usually panic and try to invent features. Mondelez did the opposite.

Snickers had nuts and nougat. 5 Star had caramel. Formulations could not be altered without hurting margins. So Ogilvy looked at the actual physical usage of the product. The heavy caramel chew forces a pause—you cannot easily talk or multitask while eating it.

That physical constraint became the campaign: Eat 5 Star, Do Nothing. Instead of competing on nut count or calories, 5 Star claimed the emotional state of unbothered procrastination.

Positioning is not about inventing features you do not have. It is about giving a name to the behavior your product already causes.

Send this to a brand lead who is trying to out-feature a competitor instead of owning a consumer state.
```
