## Caption — copy from here

```
Analyzing why this ad worked for Paper Boat shows how positioning beat established beverage giants.

In 2013, Hector Beverages entered the Indian beverage market dominated by Real and Tropicana. Both incumbents competed on health claims, added vitamins, and fruit juice percentage.

Paper Boat bypassed that feature war entirely.

They introduced traditional Indian drinks like Aam Panna and Jaljeera in squeezable pouches. By anchoring the brand to childhood nostalgia rather than health metrics, they removed the product from direct price comparison. Paper Boat priced at ₹30 for 200ml while standard tetra paks retailed at ₹20 for 200ml.

The packaging reinforced the positioning. Flexible pouches felt artisanal compared to industrial cartons. Back-of-pack micro-copy carried short stories about paper boats and summer vacations. Initial distribution prioritized airlines and corporate hubs where target consumers paid for convenience.

When you compete on features, you fight on price per milliliter. When you compete on memory, you own the category.
```
